### Closed-end credit disclosures: research only

On 2026-11-10 the full CFPB HTML pages for [§1026.17](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/17/) and [§1026.18](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/18/) were accessible and each identified itself as the current regulation; the official interpretations on those pages were also available. CFPB [§1026.2](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/2/), [§1026.3](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/3/) and [§1026.4](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/4/) were accessible. The full Florida Senate 2026 compilation pages for [§520.02](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/520.02) and [§520.07](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/520.07) were accessible; direct requests to the Florida Legislature's alternate `leg.state.fl.us` links failed. The federal pages are current-version views rather than a frozen historical determination. The 2026 state compilation is not, by itself, proof that every provision was effective for a particular deal. The history of §520.07 cites [ch. 2024-142](https://laws.flrules.org/2024/142), a directly accessible session law whose stated effective date is October 1, 2024; that date must not be applied indiscriminately to the section's older disclosure provisions. Provision-specific effective dates, subsequent changes and transition rules remain to be verified for any transaction.

- **Transaction scope:** Federal coverage, creditor identity and consumer-purpose versus other credit, exemptions, closed-end status, credit sale versus loan, and special transaction rules require fact-specific review under §§1026.1–.3 and .17–.18. Florida §520.02 separately defines retail buyers, sellers and covered contracts/transactions. A BHPH label, an undecided deal type, a deal draft or a proposed schedule does not establish coverage or exemption. Identify all parties, dates, legal obligations, vehicle, jurisdiction and terms with counsel before any determination.
- **Distinct figures:** When applicable, §1026.18(b), (d), (e) and (h) describe amount financed as net credit provided; finance charge as a dollar cost of credit; APR as the annualized cost of credit; and total of payments as scheduled payments, respectively. These are separate disclosures, not interchangeable amounts. The provision has transaction-specific exceptions, including for certain very small finance charges and single-payment transactions; Florida requirements must be reviewed independently. The amount financed is not automatically the draft worksheet balance, and the stated contract interest rate is not automatically the APR. Research includes no calculation or classification of charges.
- **Other selected topics:** Section 1026.18 also addresses creditor identification, amount-financed itemization, payment count, amounts and timing, total sale price for applicable credit sales, security interest, prepayment and late-payment terms. These are examples, not an exhaustive applicability matrix. Review prepaid finance charges, financed taxes and fees, optional products and insurance, deferred down payments, trade allowance and lien payoff, irregular periods and all payment timing with a finance specialist; unknown facts stay unknown.
- **Timing and Florida itemization:** Section 1026.17 generally requires disclosures before consummation in a clear written form the consumer may keep, subject to stated exceptions and electronic-delivery rules. Section 1026.2(a)(13) describes consummation as the point of contractual obligation on the credit transaction; applicable law determines when that occurs. Florida §520.07(1) describes completion, signing and delivery for covered contracts, including a copy or separate disclosure statement before consummation. Its subsections (2)–(3) address selected payment figures and a **separate written itemization** of amount financed; the provision also describes a limited federal-compliance deeming rule while retaining that itemization requirement. Do not assume a federal option to provide itemization on request alone satisfies the state provision. Counsel must determine applicable timing, itemization, form, recipients, delivery evidence and exceptions for each workflow.
- **Unresolved release gates:** Counsel and an independent finance specialist must review current and historical effective dates, applicable transaction categories, creditor roles, classification and independent calculation references, model/template rights, disclosure formatting, prepayment, estimates and redisclosure, signing, E-SIGN consent where relevant, copies and retention. Registry `fl-research-2026-11-10-v6` identifies this research update only. It does not select a form, decide legal applicability, calculate disclosures, or enable real credit behavior. Older packet artifacts remain frozen; new packets require a fresh preview after catalog changes.

### FTC Buyers Guide: research only

Checked 2026-11-07 against [16 CFR Part 455](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-455) (eCFR displayed Title 16 current through 2026-11-05), the [FTC dealer guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/dealers-guide-used-car-rule), the [FTC revised-rule FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/answering-dealers-questions-about-revised-used-car-rule), and the [FTC fillable form page](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/buyers-guide-fillable-form). The latter is dated November 2016; this is its publication-page date, not proof that a later revision cannot exist. The FAQ identifies January 28, 2017 as the amended rule's effective date and a one-year transition for old stock. Railet's research catalog version is separate from any official form version.

- **Source and reuse:** The FTC offers a downloadable fillable guide and says dealers may generate guides by computer, subject to the required wording, type, format and other rule conditions. The rule specifies black ink on white stock of at least 11 by 7¼ inches. This does not establish blanket rights for every redistribution, modification, translation or third-party asset; investigate those separately before packaging a form.
- **Scope and exceptions:** Section 455.1 defines covered dealers and vehicles; the FTC describes the dealer threshold as selling or offering more than five used vehicles in 12 months. Its guide identifies exceptions including certain bank, employee and lease sales, motorcycles, agricultural equipment, and vehicles sold only for scrap or parts with the specified title/salvage steps. The vehicle definition includes weight and size limits; previous title status alone does not determine whether a vehicle is used. The FTC identifies Maine and Wisconsin as state exemptions; their state requirements may still apply. Consignment and public auctions can be covered; auctions closed to consumers are excluded according to the dealer guide. These are research points, not a Railet coverage decision.
- **Display and delivery:** For covered circumstances, §455.2 and the FTC guide describe display before offering or permitting purchase inspection, prominently with both sides readable; a guide removed for a test drive must be replaced afterward. Section 455.3 and the FTC guide require the original or an accurate copy reflecting final agreed disclosures at sale. The final guide's information is incorporated into the sales contract and overrides contrary provisions; the rule specifies contract disclosure language. Warranty choices and changes need fact-specific review.
- **Language:** Section 455.5 requires the prescribed Spanish window form and contract disclosures when a sale is conducted in Spanish. The FTC dealer guide discusses posting the Spanish guide before display or offer in that situation. Review when that condition is met and any other applicable language or accessibility duties; do not infer language from a customer's identity.

**Open before production:** Counsel must review Florida-specific warranty choices and other state duties, exact transaction and vehicle coverage, exemptions, Spanish and mixed-language practices, permitted form use and version monitoring, paper versus electronic display/delivery and applicable consent, plus retention and evidence requirements. The supplied sources do not resolve those implementation questions here. No applicability decision, actual Buyers Guide generation, delivery or live workflow is enabled by this research.

Cash inventory begins with title, potential Buyers Guide, sale agreement, title-brand review and odometer/title reassignment; trade documentation appears when recorded. BHPH adds research into retail installment and credit disclosures. Undecided or other deal types retain credit review rather than assuming cash. These are investigation topics, not an exhaustive pack or determination that any item applies. Counsel must review the transaction matrix and special cases before it governs production.
